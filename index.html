Five cash-on-ramp use cases the 250K network unlocks

Use case 1 · diaspora cash-out

Recipient gets stablecoin → walks in → walks out with local cash

Pakistani recipient in Karachi receives USDC, scans QR at any of 250K SBI-affiliated outlets, walks out with PKR cash. No bank account required. KYC on the customer side; agent verifies code. This is the slide that sells to Kitao — the only credible answer to Kraken×MoneyGram.

Use case 2 · cash-in for stablecoin holding

Indonesian worker walks in → walks out holding USDC in app

Customer who doesn't trust IDR (or wants USD savings) walks into local SBI agent, deposits IDR cash, gets equivalent USDC in their Fasset wallet. Bypasses banking restrictions on FX accounts. Solves the dollarisation use case for emerging-market savers.

Proposed deal architecture

The term sheet, opinionated.

The structure that lands. Defensible on both sides. Asymmetrically rewarding to Fasset because Fasset carries the heavier integration risk. Click each clause to expand.

Headline structure

"Strategic Stablecoin Settlement & Cash Network Partnership" — Fasset provides exclusive stablecoin settlement infrastructure for SBI Remit's outbound, inbound, and B2B cross-border products. SBI Remit grants Fasset programmatic access to the 250,000-agent cash on/off-ramp network. SBI Remit retains customer relationship + compliance ownership. Revenue shared on a tiered take-rate model. Initial term 5 years with renewal. Optional minority equity investment by SBI Holdings into Fasset.

01
Parties & structure
+

Principal parties: Fasset (operating entity TBD per corridor) and SBI Remit Co., Ltd.

Structure type: Strategic commercial partnership with optional Joint Marketing Co. for the Japan-inbound product line.

  • Strategic Multi-year exclusive arrangement for Japan-anchored stablecoin remittance corridors. Both parties agree not to enter equivalent deals with direct competitors of the other for a defined period.
  • Commercial Fasset provides white-label stablecoin settlement infrastructure to SBI Remit. SBI Remit retains customer-facing brand, app, support, compliance ownership.
  • JV Separately incorporated Joint Marketing Co. — 60% SBI Remit / 40% Fasset — focused on the Japan-inbound product (the net-new revenue line).
  • Network SBI Remit grants Fasset access to the 250K-endpoint agent network for stablecoin cash on/off-ramp, governed by separate Network Access Agreement (see §6).
02
Term & exclusivity
+
  • Initial 5-year initial term from commercial launch.
  • Renewal Automatic 3-year renewals unless either party gives 12-month notice of non-renewal.
  • Exclusivity SBI Remit grants Fasset exclusivity on stablecoin settlement infrastructure for SBI Remit's products; Fasset grants SBI Remit exclusivity in Japan for stablecoin-rail consumer/SME remittance partnerships.
  • Network exclusivity The 250K agent network is accessed exclusively by Fasset (and its sub-licensed partners) for stablecoin on/off-ramp purposes during the term.
  • Carve-outs Each party retains rights to existing products and partnerships; the exclusivity applies prospectively to the partnership scope only.
03
Scope & territory
+

04
Economics — pricing & revenue share
+

Wholesale pricing model: Fasset charges SBI Remit a wholesale per-transaction settlement fee. SBI Remit sets retail pricing for end-customers. The spread between retail and wholesale is SBI Remit's gross margin.

Wholesale fee structure (synthetic):

  • Tier 1 $0–$50M/month volume: 25 bps wholesale fee on flow
  • Tier 2 $50–$250M/month volume: 18 bps wholesale fee
  • Tier 3 $250M+/month volume: 12 bps wholesale fee
  • Floor Minimum monthly fee equivalent to $250K to ensure Fasset infrastructure cost coverage

Inbound-to-Japan JV revenue share: 60% SBI Remit / 40% Fasset on profit, after both parties recover allocated direct costs.

B2B treasury revenue share: 70% SBI Remit / 30% Fasset (SBI Remit owns customer relationship and sales effort).

250K agent cash on-ramp revenue share: 60% SBI Remit / 40% Fasset on platform-level revenue. SBI Remit pays its agents from its share. Take-rate target 1.0–1.5% all-in (split between parties + agent commission of ~50bps).

Routing fees on any-to-any flows transiting Japan: 50/50 split.

05
Equity & investment
+

Optional minority investment: SBI Holdings (or SBI VC Trade) takes a strategic minority stake in Fasset, capped at 9.99% to avoid consolidation, at a valuation tied to Fasset's most recent priced round + 15% strategic premium.

Investment quantum: $25M–$50M target, mirroring the structure used for SBI's Circle investment ($25M IPO + $25M Shinsei Bank).

Use of proceeds: Fasset uses proceeds for Japan integration build, JPYSC custody architecture, B2B product development, and Asia expansion (including Tier 2/3 agent enablement build).

SBI Remit equity in JV: 60% in the Japan-inbound Joint Marketing Co.

Lock-up: 3-year lock-up for SBI Holdings on Fasset shares. Standard pre-emption rights; Fasset board observer seat for SBI nominee.

06
Agent network access & KYP
+

Network Access Agreement: Separate but linked agreement governing Fasset's access to SBI Remit's 250,000-endpoint cash agent network for stablecoin on/off-ramp.

  • Activation tiers Tier 1 (2,500 agents) live by month 6; Tier 2 (15,000) by month 18; Tier 3 (37,500) by month 36.
  • KYP standard Both parties agree to the eight-dimension KYP framework. SBI Remit performs primary KYP on each agent; Fasset performs supplementary checks (sanctions, on-chain risk).
  • Agent commissions Per-transaction commission paid by Fasset/SBI from take-rate. Floor: 30bps to agent; ceiling: 80bps depending on country. SBI Remit owns the agent contractual relationship.
  • Float & settlement Each agent maintains cash float and stablecoin counter-balance. Net settlement T+1. SBI Remit assumes counterparty risk on agent failures up to ¥1M per incident; beyond that 50/50 split.
  • Agent compliance All agents subject to ongoing AML monitoring. Both parties have audit rights. Termination right for breach.

+
  • Uptime 99.95% of Fasset settlement infrastructure (≤4.4 hours downtime/year).
  • Settlement time 95% of transactions settled in <5 minutes; 99% in <30 minutes.
  • Agent on-ramp latency Cash-in to USDC credited: median <90 seconds. 95th percentile <3 minutes.
  • FX rate guarantee Spread bands published in advance; deviations require 48-hour notice unless market-driven.
  • Customer support Tier 1 response within 4 business hours, Tier 2 escalation within 12 business hours.
  • Penalties SLA breach >0.5% in any month: wholesale fee credit equal to 25% of monthly fee. Persistent breach (3+ months in 12): SBI Remit termination right.

Customer ownership & data
+

Customer relationship: SBI Remit owns the customer at all times. Fasset is the back-end settlement layer. End-customers do not interact with Fasset interfaces unless mutually agreed.

Data ownership: Customer data sits on SBI Remit systems. Fasset receives only the minimum data needed for transaction processing and AML/KYC handoff (Travel Rule).

Cross-marketing: Each party can introduce its customers to the other's products only via mutually agreed playbook (e.g., Fasset introducing UAE/Indonesia customers to "send to Japan via SBI Remit" inbound flow). No customer-data sharing without explicit consent.

Privacy & data protection: Both parties comply with APPI (Japan), GDPR (EU corridors), DPDP Act (India), and equivalent regimes per corridor. Data residency where required (e.g., Indonesia, India).

Marketing rights: Each party can publicise the partnership in pre-agreed communications (joint press releases, conference appearances). Co-branding governed by separate Trademark Licence.

Regulatory & compliance
+

Licensing responsibility: SBI Remit holds and maintains all Japan-side licences (FTSP, MoneyGram correspondent, etc.). Fasset holds and maintains all corridor-side licences (VARA UAE, BVI, Indonesia, Pakistan PVARA pending, etc.).

  • AML/KYC SBI Remit performs customer KYC; Fasset performs wallet/address screening + on-chain analytics. Joint suspicious activity escalation framework.
  • Travel Rule Both parties implement FATF Travel Rule (R.16). Counterparty info exchanged via mutually agreed protocol (TRP, Sumsub, or equivalent).
  • Sanctions Real-time screening against OFAC, UN, EU, MoF Japan lists. Ongoing monitoring for PEP exposure.
  • Reporting Each party files local regulatory reporting independently. Joint reporting required for partnership-level disclosures (e.g., JFSA notifications about JPYSC issuance flows).
  • Audit cooperation Both parties cooperate fully with each other's regulators upon valid request. Pre-notification where legally permitted.
  • Regulatory change If a regulatory change materially affects either party's ability to perform, parties meet within 14 days to agree mitigation. Termination right if unworkable.
10
Risk allocation & indemnities
+

Stablecoin de-peg risk allocation: If USDC, JPYSC, or other in-flight stablecoin de-pegs >50bps for >30 minutes, Fasset bears the first 50bps of customer-facing impact; beyond that, parties share 50/50 up to a per-incident cap of $5M, after which loss falls on the party in possession of the affected stablecoin.

  • Operational losses Each party bears its own operational errors. Misdirected/lost transactions: party at fault makes the customer whole.
  • Cyber / security Each party indemnifies the other for losses arising from breach of its own systems. Joint incident response playbook required.
  • Agent failures See §6 — SBI Remit assumes agent counterparty risk up to ¥1M per incident; beyond that 50/50.
  • Regulatory fines Fines arising from one party's non-compliance: that party bears them. Joint compliance failures: 50/50.
  • IP infringement Each party warrants its own technology is unencumbered. Mutual IP indemnification with standard exclusions.
  • Liability cap Aggregate liability capped at greater of (a) $25M or (b) 12 months of fees paid/payable. Carve-outs for fraud, gross negligence, IP indemnification, regulatory penalties caused by wilful breach.
  • Insurance Each party maintains professional indemnity, cyber, and crime coverage at industry-standard levels for the duration of the term.

+

Termination triggers:

  • Convenience Either party may terminate without cause with 12 months' notice after end of initial 5-year term.
  • Material breach Termination on 60-day notice if breach not cured.
  • Insolvency Immediate termination on bankruptcy, administration, or analogous event.
  • Change of control Either party may terminate within 90 days of the other's change of control by a competitor (defined list, e.g., MoneyGram, Wise, Western Union, Ripple Payments retail unit, etc.).
  • Regulatory Either party may terminate if regulatory change makes performance illegal or materially uneconomic, with 30-day notice.
  • SLA persistent breach Per §7, persistent SLA failure gives SBI Remit termination right.

Exit mechanics:

  • Customer migration plan agreed at term-sheet stage. SBI Remit retains all customer data; Fasset assists with reasonable cooperation for 12 months post-termination.
  • In-flight transactions completed; new transactions cease per agreed cut-over date.
  • Equity unwind: SBI Holdings retains right to sell Fasset shares per standard pre-emption / drag-along.
  • Agent network: SBI Remit retains ownership of all agent relationships; Fasset's stablecoin technology de-installed from agents per agreed schedule.
  • Post-termination non-compete: 12-month restriction on Fasset entering competing partnership with named competitors of SBI Remit in Japan (and vice versa).

  • Tier 2: Steering Committee (15 business days)
  • Tier 3: CEO-to-CEO mediation (30 days)
  • Tier 4: Binding arbitration under SIAC rules, seat Singapore, English language, 3 arbitrators. Governing law: Singapore (neutral, both parties accept).

Negotiation philosophy

What to give. What to hold.

Give: Generous revenue share on inbound JV (60/40 SBI). Customer ownership entirely to SBI Remit. JP licensing accommodation. SBI's preferred governance cadence. Agent network counterparty risk to SBI side (they own the relationships).

Hold: Wholesale fee floor (so volume risk doesn't kill margin). Exclusivity in Japan inbound stablecoin remittance. Liability cap with carve-outs. Settlement infrastructure exclusivity for SBI Remit. Network Access Agreement structured so Fasset has clear, programmatic access to all 250K agents (subject to tiered activation), not gated case-by-case.

Negotiable buffer: Equity quantum, lock-up duration, board observer seat scope, B2B revenue split (currently 70/30 SBI; could move to 65/35 if SBI demands). The agent activation cadence (Tier 1/2/3 timing) is the biggest planning risk; build flexibility there.
