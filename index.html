01
Partnership structure · cooperative, not joint-venture

A cooperative commercial agreement, not a joint-venture entity. Initial term: 5 years Renewable Exclusivity tiered

  • Fasset operates the settlement layer under contract; agreed SLA, defined uptime, and operational metrics

  • Exclusivity for Fasset as SBI Remit's primary stablecoin settlement provider for new corridors
  • Termination clauses tied to gate failures, regulatory events, or material adverse change
02
Phased rollout · explicit go/no-go gates

Each phase has defined success criteria. Either party can decline to proceed at the next gate without penalty.

  • Gate 1 · Week 12 — Three pilot corridors live, customer NPS maintained, settlement-time benchmarks hit, regulatory go-ahead confirmed
  • Gate 2 · Month 9 — 25+ corridors live, B2B treasury product in pilot, customer adoption metrics validated
  • Gate 3 · Month 18 — Full corridor coverage operational, Tier 1 agents activated, mutual decision to scale to Phase 4
  • If a gate is not met, the partnership pauses without dissolution; remediation period agreed before re-evaluation
03
Revenue mechanics · share by line

Revenue split is line-specific, reflecting the relative contribution and cost-base of each side per product.

  • Outbound remittance — SBI Remit retains majority share (customer relationship); Fasset earns a settlement-layer fee per transaction
  • Inbound to Japan — Net-new product; majority share to SBI Remit (regulatory perimeter); Fasset earns origination fee
  • B2B treasury — Joint-build product; revenue split closer to even, structured around the relative cost-base contribution
  • Routing fees — Net-new line; structured share reflecting Tokyo as the routing node
  • JPYSC distribution — Strategic line; bespoke arrangement aligned with JPYSC issuer commercial terms
04
Operational scope · who does what

Clear demarcation of responsibilities. Each side operates within its strength.

  • SBI Remit — Customer-facing app, brand, Japan-side regulatory compliance, agent relationships, customer support
  • Fasset — Settlement layer, corridor compliance abroad, on-chain operations, virtual account infrastructure, custodial layer
  • Joint — Product development, KPI tracking, regulator engagement, fraud and disputes
  • Defined service levels for each operational responsibility, with credits for breach
05
IP and brand

Each side retains its own IP. The integration layer is jointly developed.

  • SBI Remit retains all customer-facing brand IP — name, app, marketing assets
  • Fasset retains all settlement-layer IP — OWN Network architecture, virtual account systems
  • Integration-layer code (APIs connecting the two) is jointly licensed for the partnership term
  • Customer data ownership remains with SBI Remit; Fasset operates as data processor under contract
06
Agent network access · 250K activation framework

The 250,000-agent network is SBI Remit's strategic asset. Fasset accesses it under a controlled framework.

  • Tier 1 (2,500 flagship agents) activates in Phase 1; tiers 2 and 3 follow on success metrics
  • SBI Remit retains agent contracts, training, and relationship management
  • Fasset enables stablecoin transaction types on activated agents; provides technical training
  • Activation commissions earned by agents are SBI Remit's commercial responsibility
  • No agent is exposed to crypto-native UX; the stablecoin layer is operationally invisible to agents
07
Regulatory framework · joint engagement

Both sides hold their own regulatory perimeters; engagement with regulators is coordinated.

  • JFSA and Japan-side regulators: SBI Remit-led, with joint technical input from Fasset
  • Corridor-side regulators: Fasset-led under existing licensing in 8 jurisdictions, with SBI Remit awareness
  • Joint regulatory committee meets quarterly to align on emerging developments
  • Travel Rule, AML, KYC mechanics jointly defined and operated
08
Capital commitment · contained at each stage

Capital exposure for SBI Remit is structured to be modest at each gate, with scale-up tied to validated metrics.

  • Phase 1 capital commitment: integration build cost only — modest, predominantly internal FTE time
  • Phase 2: marginal growth in operational capacity; no major capex
  • Phase 3: B2B product development costs; agent activation Tier 1
  • Phase 4: Tier 2/3 agent rollout; JPYSC scale-up if launched
  • No capital commitment beyond a gate without explicit gate approval
09
Performance KPIs · review cadence

Quarterly review of partnership performance against agreed KPIs. Defined remediation paths if metrics drift.

  • Customer-side: NPS, transaction success rate, settlement time, customer growth %
  • Operational: pre-funding capital utilisation, reconciliation overhead, FTE efficiency
  • Commercial: revenue by line, gross margin, agent activation cadence, JPYSC distribution volume
  • Regulatory: incident rate, audit findings, regulator satisfaction
  • KPI deviations of more than 20% from agreed targets trigger remediation discussion at next quarterly review
10
Exit and termination provisions

Both sides have defined exit options at each gate and during operations.

  • Termination at gate failure: no penalty, mutual remediation period before re-evaluation
  • Termination for cause: defined material breach triggers, with cure period
  • Termination for convenience: 12-month notice from year 3 onwards, with run-off transition
  • Customer data and operational continuity guaranteed through any transition
  • IP rights revert per section 05; revenue rights tail off through transition period
11
Dispute resolution and governance

Joint partnership board governs commercial decisions; defined escalation path for disputes.

  • Joint governance committee meets quarterly; senior representation from both sides
  • Operational issues handled at working-group level; commercial issues escalate to governance committee
  • Dispute resolution: mediation first; arbitration in Singapore under SIAC rules if unresolved
  • Choice of law: Singapore for the partnership agreement; corridor-specific operations governed by local law
12
Confidentiality and announcement

Standard confidentiality on terms; coordinated approach to public announcements.

  • Term sheet and definitive agreement remain confidential; mutual non-disclosure
  • Phase 1 launch announcement coordinated and timed for both sides' communication strategy
  • Customer-facing messaging joint-approved; emphasis on improved customer outcomes
  • Trade press, regulatory disclosures, and analyst engagement run through joint comms protocol
The next step

From here, two parallel tracks.

Following acceptance in principle, two workstreams begin in parallel. The commercial track moves the term sheet to definitive agreement — typically eight to twelve weeks of legal and structural work, much of which can begin immediately. The technical track begins integration scoping with confirmed pilot corridors (Vietnam, Indonesia, Philippines) so that signing day and Phase 1 launch sit within the same quarter. The first three corridors live ninety days from kick-off is a credible target, not an aspiration.
